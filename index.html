06Operating doctrine

Analyze. Structure. Activate. Sustain.

01 / Analyze→

Find the real friction

Map member behaviour, dead ends, staff load, onboarding gaps and the reasons people stop showing up. Fix the actual system, not the symptoms.

02 / Structure→

Build the machinery

Channels, roles, governance, progression, security, staff ownership and operating docs — designed as one connected system.

03 / Activate→

Create a reason to return

Launch momentum, first interactions, events, feedback loops and outreach that turn a server from technically open into socially alive.

04 / Sustain↻

Make it survive you

Train the team, distribute ownership, measure retention and keep improving until the community can run without one person holding it together.

07QuestionsStraight answers

Before you book the call.

What does Joey do?

Joey is a Discord community architect and author. He launches new communities, repairs dying ones and builds the staff teams that run them: server setup and security, onboarding, governance, events, growth and outreach, and the writing that holds it together.

Who does Joey work with?

Game studios building a player community, Kickstarter creators who need a home for their backers, agencies that need someone to own the Discord side of a client launch, and community owners whose server has gone quiet. His work includes a paid client project for Guildstew's Kickstarter community, the Project Aurora roleplay community and Crimson Network.

What does a project look like?

Three steps: a review of your community or plan, an agreed scope, then the build or the ongoing management. For Guildstew that meant onboarding, backer access, support tickets, feedback forums and staff operations in one server, handed over with a staff playbook and a 14-day plan.

Can Joey fix a community that's going quiet?

Yes. A repair starts with an audit of where members drop out, then rebuilds channels, retention and reactivation loops, events and a growth and partnership plan around how people actually behave.

Does Joey only set servers up, or run them too?

Both. On Project Aurora he was Executive Director on the Aurora Board and led the whole staff team. On Crimson Network he built and trained a staff team that kept running after he moved on.

What results can Joey show?

Project Aurora went from 9 messages on day one to 2,508 messages a day by day fourteen. Crimson Network peaked at about 1,400 members. Site-202 reached 4,000+ messages on launch day and 100 members in its first week. Every figure comes from server message logs, member counts and dated announcement records.
